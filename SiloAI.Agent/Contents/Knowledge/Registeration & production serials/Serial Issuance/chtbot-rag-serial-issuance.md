## 2. Differences Between Core Serial Issuance Concepts

### 2.1. Serial

A serial is an identifier assigned to an individual product unit or a group of product units. Depending on the configuration and operational scenario, one serial may represent either one product unit or multiple units of the same product.

Therefore, the number of issued serials does not necessarily equal the number of product units represented by those serials.

### 2.2. Requested Number of Serials

The requested number of serials specifies how many independent serials or identifiers must be issued.

For example, if the requested number of serials is 3, the system must create three serials. The number of product units covered by each serial is a separate matter.

### 2.3. Number of Product Units Covered by Each Serial

This value specifies how many product units are represented by each serial.

Example:

- Requested number of serials: 1
- Number of product units covered by each serial: 15
- Length/area per product unit: 0.4 square meters
- Total number of product units: 15
- Total area covered by the serial: 6 square meters

In this case, only one serial is issued, but that serial represents 15 product units with a combined area of 6 square meters.

### 2.4. Total Requested Quantity

The total requested quantity is calculated from the combined quantity of the units covered by the requested serials.

If the requested number of serials is 3, the number of product units covered by each serial is 1, and the area per product unit is 7.5 meters:

- Total number of product units: 3
- Area per unit: 7.5 meters
- Total requested area: 22.5 meters

These concepts must be understood independently because changing the number of serials, the number of product units covered by each serial, or the quantity per unit produces different results.

## 3. Serial Issuance Process and Product Information in Silo

### 3.1. General Steps

The general serial issuance process in the Windows application may include the following steps:

1. Receive or specify the operation code.
2. Select the product.
3. Specify the number of serials or the requested quantity.
4. Complete additional information and dynamic fields, if required.
5. Specify operational information, such as the source document, production line, production shift, or warehouse, if used in the relevant scenario.
6. Execute the serial issuance request.
7. View the created serials and continue with printing or registration according to the workflow in use.

The exact sequence and the presence of all steps depend on the form configuration and operational scenario.

### 3.2. Product Selection Methods

In the Windows application, a product can be selected through the product search form. The user searches for and selects the required product, and its information is displayed for the next steps.

Another method is to select the product through a source document. In this case, the user first selects the document and then chooses the required product from the document's line items.

With the second method, the system can restrict product selection to items listed in the source document and prevent the selection of products that are not included, provided that the relevant document control is enabled and enforced by the form.

### 3.3. Single-Product and Multi-Product Modes

The Windows application supports two serial issuance patterns:

**Single-Product Mode**

In this pattern, the user specifies an operation code, selects one product, specifies the quantity, and proceeds to the next stage of serial issuance.

This pattern is suitable for scenarios in which each request is focused on a single product.

**Multi-Product Mode**

In this pattern, the user can enter multiple different products, together with the requested quantity for each product, in a single request.

For example, one request may specify five serials for the first product and three serials for the second. If the operation completes successfully, eight serials are created for two different products.

Removing entered items and confirming the complete request are also supported in this pattern, as described.

The choice between the two modes depends on the software configuration and process requirements.

### 3.4. Fixing the Quantity at One

In some scenarios, each data entry must correspond to only one serial because the additional information entered belongs to that specific unit and must not be shared across multiple serials.

In such cases, the form can be configured to keep the quantity fixed at one and prevent the user from increasing it.

This restriction is suitable for scenarios in which separate, unit-specific information is recorded for each product unit.

Fixing the quantity at one is a configuration-dependent capability and must not be treated as mandatory behavior for all serial issuance forms.

### 3.5. Methods of Generating or Obtaining Serial Numbers

The method used to generate or obtain a serial is independent of the platform on which the operation runs. The methods discussed include:

- **Sequential Numeric Numbering:** Serials are generated from a numeric sequence, such as `1000, 1001, 1002`. Gaps may occur between numbers, so continuity must not be assumed without verification.
- **Automatic Identifier:** An identifier generated automatically by the system is used to construct or provide the serial. An automatically generated identifier does not necessarily mean sequential numbering without gaps.
- **Custom Formula:** The serial is generated according to a project-specific pattern, such as a combination of product code, date, location identifier, or counter. The formula components and final format must be determined from the actual project configuration.
- **Using an Existing Number:** A product may already have a number, such as an IMEI or a serial generated by a source system. Depending on the project logic, this number may be used unchanged or combined with other data. The presence of an input number alone does not prove that the same value will become the final registered serial.

The serial generation method and the location where generation takes place are two separate matters. A serial may be generated within Silo, on an Android device with local generation logic, or in another system and then transferred to Silo. A project's custom formula must not be generalized to other projects.

### 3.6. Product Profile, Serial-Specific Information, and Dynamic Fields

Three categories of information must be distinguished during serial issuance:

- **General Product Information:** Information defined for the product as a whole and potentially shared by multiple units of that product.
- **Serial-Specific Information:** Information belonging to a particular unit, package, or individual record. Examples include chassis number, body number, VIN, license plate, or order number, if the project defines these fields.
- **Operational Information:** Data required to perform an operation, such as production line, production hall, shift, or warehouse. These data are not necessarily part of the product's general information or the serial's permanent attributes.

Dynamic fields are used to collect information that varies according to the product, serial, or operation. Examples may include width, grammage, fuel type, application type, chassis number, or VIN. The actual field list depends on the form and project.

Field behavior may also vary: a field may be editable by the user, display-only, automatically populated, or calculated from other data. It must not be assumed that every displayed field is editable or exists in every project. Information may be completed during serial issuance or at a separate stage.

### 3.7. General Serial Issuance Workflow in the Web Form

According to the user guide configuration described, the serial issuance form is located under «چاپ / مدیریت». The displayed title or menu location may vary depending on the version, menu structure, and project.

Where the relevant fields are available, the general workflow may include the following steps:

1. Select a product from the defined product list or from the items in a source document.
2. Enter or select production information, such as production line, hall, or shift, if required.
3. Select the destination warehouse if the workflow requires warehouse information.
4. Enter source document information if it is mandatory.
5. Specify the product quantity and requested number of serials according to the form's capabilities.
6. Complete product-specific or serial-specific fields.
7. Submit the request and verify the actual operation result.

These steps are general and may not all be present in every form, or their order may differ. If the form is not visible in the menu, the menu structure and user access permissions should be checked. The absence of a menu option alone does not prove that the capability is unavailable throughout the system.

The serial issuance form is organized into three main sections:

This section contains the operation-related information and controls required for serial issuance.

- Depending on the operation configuration, one or multiple serials can be issued in a single operation.

**Fixed Requested Serial Quantity**

- Silo can be configured to restrict the requested number of serials to a fixed value.
- For example, a project may require exactly one serial per operation and prevent the user from changing the requested quantity.
- This restriction depends on project requirements and is configured by the project's support team.
- This behavior must not be assumed to apply to every project.

**Serial Printing**

- The form provides printing functionality for issued serials.
- The user selects the required print template and executes the printing operation.
- Available templates depend on the project's configuration.

## 4. Using a Source Document for Serial Issuance

### 4.1. Concept of a Source Document

A source document is a reference that defines the products and permitted quantities for serial issuance.

The document type depends on the organization's process. Conceptual examples include:

- Production plan
- Shipment packing list (Packing List)
- Warehouse receipt
- Other documents that specify products and expected quantities

Therefore, a source document is not necessarily a production document. In one scenario, it may be a production plan; in another, it may be a goods receipt or a shipment item list.

### 4.2. Restricting Product Selection

If source document controls are enabled, the user can select only products included in the document's line items.

If the user selects a product that is not listed in the document, the system can reject the operation and prevent serial issuance for that product.

The purpose of this control is to reduce errors caused by similar products, a large variety of items, or incorrect operator selections.

### 4.3. Restricting the Issuance Quantity

A source document can also control the permitted issuance quantity.

For example, if a document line specifies 20 units of a product, serial issuance must not cause the covered quantity to exceed the document's permitted limit, provided that this control is enabled in the workflow.

The user can issue the quantity in stages, such as two units first, followed by four units and then ten units. The system must control the issued quantity and remaining quantity according to the document's logic.

### 4.4. Displaying Items and Remaining Quantity

The document selection form may display information such as document details and its line items.

Item information may include:

- Product code
- Product title
- Requested quantity
- Remaining quantity to be issued

The remaining quantity indicates how much of the document's permitted quantity has not yet been issued.

Example:

- Requested document quantity: 20 units
- Quantity already issued: 12 units
- Remaining quantity: 8 units

If the remaining quantity is zero, the quantity defined for that document line has been fully consumed.

This information helps operators prevent duplicate issuance or issuance beyond the permitted quantity.

### 4.5. Source Documents and Integration with Other Systems

A source document may not have been created in Silo itself. It may be received from another system, such as a planning system, accounting system, or ERP.

In such cases, document information is imported into Silo through the available integration mechanism and can then be used in the serial issuance process.

The advantage of this approach is that product and quantity information comes from the relevant operational source, so the user does not have to re-enter all the information in the serial issuance form.

The existence of this capability does not imply automatic support for every external system. The data acquisition method depends on the defined interface and integration mechanism.

## 5. Managing Quantity, Length, and Weight During Serial Issuance

### 5.1. Difference Between Serial Count and Product Quantity

Three quantities must be distinguished during serial issuance:

1. The requested number of serials.
2. The number of product units covered by each serial.
3. The physical quantity of the product, such as length or weight.

These quantities may differ from one another.

For example, one serial may represent 15 pieces, with a combined area of six square meters recorded for those pieces.

### 5.2. Automatic Length/Area Calculation

If the number of product units and the length/area per unit are known, the system can calculate the total length/area.

Conceptual formula:

Total area = Number of product units × Area per unit

Example:

- Number of units: 15
- Area per unit: 0.4 square meters
- Total area: 6 square meters

In packaging scenarios, the number of cartons or units inside a package may affect the total area calculation.

### 5.3. Changing the Quantity Inside a Package

A default quantity may be defined for a full package, but a produced package may be incomplete.

For example:

- Default quantity per pallet: 42 cartons
- Actual quantity on an incomplete pallet: 25 cartons

If the form allows the quantity to be edited, the user can change the actual quantity from 42 to 25. The system can recalculate the total area using the product's master data and the actual quantity.

As a result, the issued serial represents a package containing 25 cartons and the corresponding quantity, rather than a complete package of 42 cartons.

This calculation depends on valid master data being available and the relevant capability being enabled in the form.

### 5.4. Calculating Weight Based on Total Weight

Silo provides a capability called «محاسبه وزن بر اساس وزن کل» (Calculate Weight Based on Total Weight). When enabled in the form settings, it can be used to distribute a total weight across multiple units.

This capability is useful in scenarios where the total net weight of a shipment is known, but the exact weight of each individual unit or bag is not.

Conceptual steps:

1. Measure the gross weight of the vehicle carrying the load.
2. Measure the weight of the empty vehicle.
3. Calculate the shipment's net weight by subtracting the empty vehicle weight from the loaded vehicle weight.
4. Determine the number of units in the shipment.
5. Divide the net weight by the number of units to calculate the average weight per unit.

Formulas:

Net shipment weight = Loaded vehicle weight − Empty vehicle weight

Average weight per unit = Net shipment weight ÷ Number of units

Example:

- Loaded vehicle weight: 47 tons
- Empty vehicle weight: 24.8 tons
- Net shipment weight: 22.2 tons
- Number of bags: 22
- Average weight per bag: approximately 1.009 tons, equivalent to about 1 ton and 9 kilograms

**Note:** The figures above are illustrative calculations only. Actual weights and the correct units of measurement must be entered when performing the operation. The calculated average weight is not necessarily the actual weight of each individual unit.

## 6. Receiving Serials from Other Systems

### 6.1. Concept of External Serial Issuance

In some scenarios, Silo is not responsible for generating the serial number. The serial is generated in another system, and its information is then transferred to Silo.

In this case, Silo records the received information in its workflow. Subsequent stages, such as printing or transferring information to tag registration, are performed according to the relevant implementation.

### 6.2. Initial Destination of Received Information

Serial information received from other systems enters the Silo workflow and can then be used in subsequent stages, such as printing or tag registration, depending on the implementation. This process may support different information acquisition methods, including:

- API-based integration
- Query-based integration
- Importing data from an Excel file

The next stage may involve printing or tag registration. The order and execution of these stages depend on the implementation and tools used; they do not necessarily mean that printing must occur before tag registration.

**Important:** Receiving serial information or preparing it for use does not mean that physical printing has taken place.

### 6.3. API-Based Integration

In the API-based method, systems communicate through a defined application programming interface.

In the described mechanism, Silo Job can call the relevant API at specified intervals, such as every minute, and transfer new data to Silo.

Silo Job is a dedicated Silo service. In this scenario, its execution does not depend on SQL Server Agent Job.

### 6.4. Query-Based Integration

In the query-based method, information is retrieved through a query or View in the source system's database.

Two implementation patterns were discussed:

- Using Silo Job to execute the data retrieval process and transfer the data to Silo
- Using SQL Server Agent Job to execute a scheduled query and transfer the information

The choice depends on the integration architecture, available permissions, and implementation settings.

### 6.5. Offline Import Through Excel

The Windows application supports importing information through Import Excel.

This capability is useful when serial information is provided as a file and is not expected to be received immediately through an API or direct database connection.

The extent of support for this method depends on the configuration and implementation of the relevant form.

## 7. Serial Issuance in the Android Version

### 7.1. Serial Issuance Forms

Two forms related to serial issuance have been described for the Android version:

- «ثبت با کد کالا»
- «ثبت با کد کالا و سریال»

These forms can also be used in offline mode. However, the capabilities available offline are not necessarily identical to those available online.

### 7.2. Offline Mode Without Local Serial Generation

In this pattern, the user submits a serial issuance request, but the device itself does not generate the final serial.

Requests are stored in the device queue and sent to the server once an online connection becomes available. The server then performs the final serial issuance.

In this mode, a serial issuance request must be distinguished from the actual generation of a serial.

### 7.3. Offline Mode with Local Serial Generation

In the other pattern, direct serial generation on the Android device is enabled.

For this purpose, the serial generation formula is provided to the device as a SQLite query. Form information, such as the product code or workshop code, is passed to the formula, and the serial is generated accordingly.

SQLite queries differ from SQL Server queries in structure and capabilities. Therefore, the formula used for offline serial generation must be designed for the SQLite environment.

### 7.4. Serial Uniqueness in Offline Mode

Maintaining serial uniqueness is essential in offline mode, especially when multiple devices operate simultaneously.

One approach is to include a distinctive identifier, such as a workshop code, in the serial structure. If this identifier is incorporated into the serial generation formula and valid, distinct values are assigned to the devices, the likelihood of collisions is reduced.

This is a design pattern, not an automatic guarantee of uniqueness under all conditions. The formula, identifier ranges, and synchronization process must be designed to prevent duplicate serials.

### 7.5. Local Device Information

In the described architecture, some information related to serials and tags can be loaded onto an Android device so that it can access the required data even when disconnected.

The details of locally stored data, synchronization, and conflict handling depend on the implementation of the relevant scenario.

### 7.6. Printing Limitations on Android

In the state described during the session, label printing through the Android version is not available as a generally operational capability.

Sending print commands directly from Android to a Bluetooth jet printer was identified as a requirement and a planned future enhancement.

Therefore, this capability must not be presented as a currently available and confirmed feature of the Android version.

## 8. Guidelines for the Agent When Answering User Questions

### 8.1. Questions About Source Documents

Explain that a source document defines the reference products and permitted quantities for serial issuance. When the relevant controls are enabled, the document can restrict product selection, enforce issuance limits, and display the remaining quantity to the operator.

Do not describe a source document exclusively as a “production plan,” because depending on the scenario, it may be a warehouse receipt, a shipment packing list, or another valid document.

### 8.2. Questions About One Serial Representing Multiple Product Units

Explain that the number of serials, the number of units covered by each serial, and the physical quantity of goods are three different concepts.

For an accurate answer, determine whether the user is asking about the number of serials, the number of component units associated with each serial, or the quantity expressed as length or weight.

### 8.3. Questions About Duplicate Serials in Offline Mode

Explain that offline generation requires an appropriate formula and measures to prevent collisions between devices. A distinctive identifier, such as a workshop code, can be part of the solution, but it does not guarantee uniqueness by itself unless the complete serial generation logic ensures that property.

## 9. Inference Limitations and Rules for Preventing Incorrect Answers

1. Generating a serial on Android does not, by itself, mean that direct printing is supported on Android.

2. One serial may represent multiple product units. Therefore, do not assume that the number of serials is equal to the number of product units.

3. The use of source documents and controls on remaining quantities must be verified against the actual configuration and implementation of the form.

4. Information received from another system may be acquired through an API, a query, or a file. Do not treat all these methods as a single integration mechanism.

5. Silo Job and SQL Server Agent Job are two different mechanisms. Their names must not be used interchangeably.

6. Calculating average weight from the total weight does not determine the actual weight of every individual unit; it produces a calculated average.

7. Configuration-dependent capabilities, such as fixing the quantity at one, calculating weight based on total weight, and enforcing source document controls, must not be presented as mandatory behavior for every form.

8. Serial issuance, creation of a print record, physical printing, RFID reading, tag registration, and warehouse entry are separate events. Completion must be confirmed only on the basis of a reliable result for the relevant operation.

9. Do not reduce offline behavior to a single pattern without verification. The device may retain a request for later processing or generate a serial locally in a specific scenario.

10. Distinguish Silo Job from SQL Server Agent Job. In the API-based integration path, Silo Job does not necessarily depend on SQL Server Agent. In the query-based path, either Silo Job or SQL Server Agent Job may be used, depending on the implementation.

11. If behavior depends on the project, version, permissions, or configuration, and reliable information is unavailable, explicitly state this limitation. Do not fill in the missing details by guessing.

## 10. Summary of Capabilities

Silo provides various methods for serial issuance, including single-product and multi-product issuance, restricting operations based on a source document, controlling the remaining quantity, configuring the number of units covered by each serial, calculating length and weight in relevant scenarios, and preserving relationships between packaging levels.

Serial information may be generated by Silo tools or received from other systems through API-based integration, query-based integration, or Excel files. In offline Android scenarios, depending on the configuration, a request may be queued for later processing by the server, or a serial may be generated locally on the device using a SQLite formula.

When answering users, the agent must always distinguish between existing capabilities, configuration-dependent capabilities, technical limitations, and planned future enhancements. Details that have not been confirmed by the available knowledge must not be presented as established facts.
