# Silo Technical Knowledge Base: Serial Generation, Printing, RFID Registration, and Warehouse Entry Logic

## 1. Document Purpose, Audience, and Scope

This document defines the conceptual and technical logic of serial number generation or provisioning, initial serial record creation, label printing, RFID identifier association, tag registration, and warehouse entry for the internal analysis of the Silo agent.

The goal is to enable the agent to distinguish between an operational request, database records, and the actual state of a product or tag, and to provide responses based on reliable documentation and actual data.

This document covers the following topics:

- Serial generation or provisioning methods and request execution channels.
- The roles of Print and Tag and the relationship between them.
- RFID identifiers or EPCs and their association with serial numbers.
- The group identifier `PrintActionId`.
- Printing and registration statuses.
- Tag registration, destination, warehouse, and inventory movement.
- The roles of `ActionType` and `ProductType`.
- Document-related controls and quality control.
- Web, online Android, offline Android, Windows, and API architectures.
- Android registration forms and dynamic fields.
- Inference limitations and the agent's response rules.

**Fundamental principle:** This document defines the knowledge model and analysis rules. Definitive details, such as actual field names, database relationships, status values, operation sequences, and the controls enabled for each project, must be verified against the actual database schema, application code, or authoritative technical documentation.

## 2. Separation of Process Stages

When analyzing the serial issuance and product registration process, the following events must be treated as separate stages.

### 2.1. Serial Generation or Provisioning

A serial number may be generated by Silo or received from an external system.

### 2.2. Initial Serial Record Creation

The serial number and its associated information are stored in the initial data structure. In the architecture described here, Print plays the primary role at this stage.

### 2.3. Physical Label Printing

The serial information is printed on a label using the appropriate print template. Creating a serial record does not, by itself, prove that physical printing has occurred.

### 2.4. RFID Identifier Acquisition or Reading

The tag identifier may be read from an RFID device or obtained from another source according to the logic of a specific project.

### 2.5. Tag Registration

The RFID identifier is associated with the corresponding serial number, and the tag record is stored in the Tag structure.

### 2.6. Movement and Warehouse Status Recording

In workflows where Silo's operational logic requires warehouse entry to be recorded, movement and destination warehouse information are also registered.

These stages may be executed within a single workflow or through several separate requests. However, they are conceptually distinct.

For example:

- Submitting a serial issuance request does not prove that all serial records were created successfully.
- The existence of a serial in Print does not prove that its physical label was printed.
- Reading an EPC does not prove that registration succeeded.
- The existence of a Tag record does not, by itself, prove that physical printing succeeded.
- Tag registration, without examining movement records, does not necessarily establish that warehouse entry has been completed.

When responding to a question, the agent must identify which stage is meant by "successful registration": serial creation, printing, tag registration, or completion of the warehouse workflow.

## 3. Serial Generation Methods and Request Execution Channels

The serial generation method and the request execution channel are two independent concepts.

### 3.1. Serial Generation or Provisioning Methods

Four main methods are described below.

**A. Sequential Serial**

Serial numbers are generated according to a numeric sequence. For example:

`1000, 1001, 1002, 1003`

The expected sequence does not necessarily imply that there will never be gaps. Concurrent requests, failed transactions, record deletion, or number-allocation logic may affect the observed sequence.

**B. Identity-Based Serial**

An Identity value may be used to provide or construct a serial number. However, Identity is fundamentally an automatically generated record identifier and must not be assumed to be the same as the product's business serial number without examining the implementation.

Identity also does not necessarily guarantee a gap-free sequence.

**C. Project-Specific Formula (Custom Formula)**

The serial number is generated using a formula defined for the project. The formula may use the product code, date, counter, prefix, suffix, or other permitted values.

Conceptual example:

`Product Code + Date + Counter`

The output may have a specified length, but the formula components, final length, and generation rules must be obtained from the documentation for that specific project.

**D. External Serial**

The serial number is received from the customer, product, or source system; for example, an identifier such as an IMEI.

The incoming serial may be used as-is or combined with the product code and other information. Therefore, the serial received from an external source is not necessarily the final serial number.

### 3.2. Request Execution Channels

A serial generation or registration request may be executed through one of the following channels:

- Web.
- Online Android.
- Offline Android.
- Windows application or kiosk.
- API.

The serial generation method must not be inferred solely from the execution channel. For example, an API may submit a request for Silo to generate a serial number, or it may transfer a serial that was already generated by the source system.

### 3.3. Hardcoded Formula vs. Configurable Formula

In some legacy implementations, the serial generation formula is hardcoded in the application. Changing the formula in such a structure may require modifying the code, building a new version, and redeploying the software.

A more configurable architecture stores the formula in the database and provides a way to define or modify it through system settings.

These two cases must not be confused:

- **Existing behavior:** What is actually implemented in the current project version.
- **Desired architecture:** A proposed design intended to improve flexibility.

The agent must not assume that all Silo versions support defining new serial formulas through the user interface.

## 4. Conceptual Structure of Print

### 4.1. Role of Print

In the architecture described here, Print is the reference for the initial information of generated or provisioned serial numbers. It may store information required by subsequent stages, such as label printing or tag registration.

The existence of a record in Print does not mean that the tag has been finally registered in Tag.

### 4.2. Information Associated with a Print Record

The available technical documentation associates the following information with the Print structure:

- Serial number.
- Product code.
- Product title or description.
- Product type.
- Quantity.
- Item count.
- Unit.
- Size.
- Short product code.
- Printing status.
- Registration status.
- EPC identifier, if recorded at this stage.

This is a conceptual list of required information. Without verifying the actual schema, it must not be treated as a definitive list of columns present in every Silo version.

### 4.3. Difference Between Quantity and Item Count

Quantity and item count may have different meanings.

For example, in a tile project:

- Quantity may represent the total area of a pallet.
- Item count may represent the number of cartons on the pallet.

The exact meaning of these values depends on the project definition and its actual fields.

The following two concepts must also be distinguished:

- **Serial count:** The number of serial records the request intends to create.
- **Quantity per serial:** The quantity of goods represented by each serial record.

For example, five serials with a quantity of 87 pieces per serial are structurally different from one serial with a quantity of 435 pieces, even if the total quantity of goods is the same.

### 4.4. Short Product Code

A short code may be used to store an abbreviated product name or identifier. For example, `SMBS` may be used in a particular project as a short code for Sodium Metabisulfite.

This example is illustrative only and must not be treated as a general rule for generating short codes in Silo.

## 5. Conceptual Structure of Tag and the Relationship Between Serial Numbers and RFID

### 5.1. Role of Tag

Tag stores records of registered tags in the system.

In the conceptual model used for analysis:

- Print stores the initial serial information.
- Tag stores registered tag records.
- The EPC or RFID identifier associates a tag with its corresponding serial number.

This distinction does not mean that implementation details are identical across all projects. The actual registration path must be verified against the code and database structure of the specific project.

### 5.2. EPC

An EPC is an identifier associated with a tag in an RFID scenario.

During some initial stages, a Print record may not yet have an EPC. In common registration workflows, the EPC is acquired, associated with the corresponding serial number, and stored as part of the tag registration data.

The presence of an EPC in a record is not sufficient, by itself, to conclude that registration was completed successfully. Its association with the serial number and the actual state of the records must also be verified.

### 5.3. Database Relationships

In the current Silo knowledge model, `tbl_Tags.Id` is an identity value for the record and must not be treated as a relationship key for other tables without supporting evidence.

For tag relationship analysis, the combination of `ProductSerial` and `TagEpc` is important. However, each specific relationship must be verified against the actual data structure used by the relevant workflow.

The agent must not create unverified database relationships based solely on similar column names or the presence of an identifier.

### 5.4. Three Independent Questions for Status Verification

When analyzing a serial number or tag, the following questions must be answered separately:

1. Does the serial information exist in Print?
2. Does a corresponding record exist in Tag?
3. Is the RFID identifier associated with the intended serial number?

When warehouse status also needs to be checked, a fourth question arises:

4. Has the expected movement or destination status been recorded in the operational data?

The answers must be extracted from actual data. The agent must not assume that all stages were executed and completed successfully at the same time.

## 6. Selecting the Appropriate Reference for Serial Generation

In the described architecture, Print is the reference for generated or allocated serial information, while Tag is the reference for registered tag records.

This distinction is important when generating serial numbers because a serial may have been generated and even printed but may not yet have been registered.

If the serial allocation logic checks only the latest serial in Tag, it may ignore serial numbers already created in Print but not yet transferred to Tag. If such an algorithm exists, it may create a risk of assigning the same serial number again.

Therefore, when reviewing or designing the serial allocation logic, the following must be established:

- What is the actual source of serial allocation?
- How are serials that have been created but not yet registered handled?
- How is serial uniqueness guaranteed?
- How are concurrent requests controlled?
- Is there a database uniqueness constraint?
- In offline mode, how is duplicate serial generation across multiple devices prevented?

**Important rule:** Using Print as the serial allocation reference is part of the architecture described here. However, the agent must compare this model with the actual serial generation algorithm in the version under investigation and must not make a definitive claim about every project without implementation-level verification.

## 7. Printing and Registration Statuses

The available technical documentation identifies the following two statuses for Print records:

- `PrintFlag`
- `RegisterFlag`

For a new serial that has not yet been printed or registered, the example status is described as follows:

```text
PrintFlag = 0
RegisterFlag = 0
EPC = Empty
```

This example indicates that a serial record may exist in Print even though printing and registration have not yet occurred.

However, before relying on these values definitively, the code or authoritative documentation must establish:

- The exact meaning of each value.
- Whether the statuses are limited to zero and one or include additional values.
- Which operations change each status.
- Whether status updates occur within the same registration transaction.
- Whether the printing status means that a print command was sent or that physical printing was confirmed.
- How the status is handled when an operation fails or a request is repeated.

The agent must not guess the precise meaning of status values or the order in which they change without reliable evidence.

## 8. Technical Lifecycle of Tag Registration

### 8.1. Conceptual Workflow

In a typical registration scenario, the conceptual workflow is as follows:

1. The serial number and its associated information exist in Print or another authoritative source.
2. The RFID identifier is acquired through a supported method.
3. The corresponding serial number is identified according to the project's rules.
4. The RFID identifier is associated with the serial number.
5. The tag record is stored in Tag.
6. The Print record and related statuses are updated, if this behavior is included in the implementation.
7. If the operational workflow requires warehouse entry, the corresponding movement record and destination are also recorded.

This is a conceptual workflow. The exact order of commands, transactions, and database operations must be extracted from the actual code.

### 8.2. Difference Between Tag Registration and Completion of Warehouse Movement

Two levels of completion must be distinguished.

**Tag registration:** Recording the tag and verifying the association between the RFID identifier and the corresponding serial number according to the project's rules.

**Completion of the warehouse entry workflow:** Completing all stages required by that workflow, which may include updating Print, recording movement, specifying a destination, and recording warehouse status.

Therefore, if a Tag record has been created but the expected movement record does not exist, tag registration may have occurred while completion of the warehouse entry workflow remains unconfirmed.

Whether all these stages are mandatory for a particular project must be established from the actual implementation.

## 9. TagMovement and Warehouse Status

In the described structure, TagMovement is used to record movements associated with a tag or product.

In some registration workflows, a movement record is created after the Tag record to represent the entry of goods into the destination warehouse.

To analyze this stage, the following must be established:

- Does the workflow in question actually create a movement record?
- What is the selected destination?
- Which serial number or tag does the movement record refer to?
- Was the movement record actually created?
- Does the record represent an entry, exit, transfer, or another event?
- Can the current physical location of the goods be inferred from this record?

The existence of a movement record, without examining the operation type and its meaning, does not by itself prove that the goods are currently located at a specific physical location.

### 9.1. Relationship Between Registration and Warehouse Entry

In the operational logic described for some Silo workflows, creating the operational record for a serial is associated with specifying a destination warehouse.

This rule must not be generalized to all versions or operation types without verification. For each workflow, it must be established whether a destination warehouse is mandatory and at which stage warehouse status is recorded.

## 10. ActionType, Destination, and Product Type

### 10.1. Role of ActionType

`ActionType` defines the type of operation in an operational workflow and may affect the source, destination, and controls applicable to that operation.

In Silo's conceptual operation model, the destination type must be distinguished from a specific destination. An operation type does not necessarily identify a particular warehouse or destination on its own.

### 10.2. Source Value `-1`

In the available technical documentation, source value `-1` is described for some operations as representing the state before registration or before a serial has been placed in a warehouse.

In such configurations, the following path may represent an initial registration or entry operation:

`-1 → Destination`

This interpretation depends on the actual configuration of the project. The agent must not assign a definitive meaning to every occurrence of `-1` across all Silo structures without checking the operation settings.

### 10.3. Relationship Between ProductType and ActionType

Product type may influence the selection of permitted operations or suitable destinations.

For example, a project may define different workflows for the following product categories:

- Finished goods → Finished Goods Warehouse.
- Raw materials → Raw Materials Warehouse.
- Semi-finished goods → The relevant warehouse for semi-finished products.

These examples are conceptual. Actual destinations, relationships, and operations must be obtained from the configuration of the specific project.

## 11. Document Controls and Quality Control

### 11.1. DocCodeSet

In the available technical documentation, `DocCodeSet` is described as a control that, when enabled, makes entering a document number mandatory for an operation.

In such a configuration, the absence of a document number may prevent the operation from being recorded.

Whether this control is enabled, its actual value, and its precise behavior must be verified against the settings of the relevant operation.

### 11.2. DocumentItemCheck

A control related to document items may be used to validate an operational request against document information. The described logic may include the following steps:

1. Validating the document number.
2. Retrieving document items from the relevant source.
3. Matching the requested product against the document items.
4. Comparing the requested quantity with the quantity permitted by the document.

For example, if the product does not exist in the document or the requested quantity exceeds the permitted quantity, the operation configuration may block further processing.

The exact control name, validation scope, and behavior must be established from the actual implementation. This document alone does not prove that all these controls are enabled for every operation or project.

### 11.3. Integration with External Systems

In some projects, document information and its items are retrieved from external systems.

In such cases, validating an issuance or registration request against an external document depends on the integration contract, the data received, and the rules of the specific project. The agent must not assume that all Silo projects use the same external system or integration method.

### 11.4. Quality Control

Operation-type settings may play a role in workflows that require quality control.

For example, the entry of finished goods into a warehouse may require quality approval or inspection, while raw material registration may follow different rules.

The agent must evaluate product type, operation type, and enabled controls using actual data. The existence of a product type alone does not prove that quality control is enabled for all operations involving that product type.

## 12. Differences Between RFID and Barcode-Based Projects

### 12.1. True RFID Projects

In a typical RFID scenario, the actual tag identifier is read from a device and associated with the corresponding serial number according to the project's logic. The tag record is then registered.

A successful identifier read confirms only that the identifier was acquired; it does not prove that registration was completed successfully.

### 12.2. Barcode-Based Projects

In some barcode-based projects, the serial number may be used as the RFID identifier within the system's data structure. In such a scenario, label printing may be accompanied by the creation of, or transfer of information to, the Tag structure.

This behavior is not universal and must not be generalized to all projects.

### 12.3. Identifying the Project Type

The project type must be determined using authoritative documentation, the identifier acquisition method, and the actual registration workflow.

The agent must not conclude that a project uses true RFID or a barcode-based approach solely from a field name, form title, or the existence of a record.

## 13. PrintActionId and Request Grouping

In the described model, `PrintActionId` is a group identifier associated with a serial issuance request or operation.

For example, if a request creates multiple serials, the related Print records may share the same PrintActionId. In some scenarios, a single request may include multiple products.

This identifier must not be confused with:

- The product serial number.
- The product code.
- The RFID identifier or EPC.
- A definitive identifier for a Tag record.

PrintActionId alone does not prove that:

- All serials in the request were created.
- All labels were printed.
- All tags were registered.
- All records in the group have the same final status.
- The warehouse operation was completed for every record.

This identifier can be used to group records associated with a request and inspect their results. The outcome of each stage must be established from the data associated with that stage.

## 14. Web, Android, Offline, and API Architecture

### 14.1. Web

A web form may collect product information, production details, serial count, quantity per serial, destination, document information, and project-specific fields.

In the described form, the access path is `Print / Management`. However, the menu name and availability may vary according to the menu structure and user permissions.

The absence of a form from a user's menu does not, by itself, prove that the form does not exist in the system.

### 14.2. Online Android

In online mode, the request is sent to the server and processed by the server-side logic.

A connection to the server does not, by itself, prove that the request succeeded or that all controls were executed. The outcome must be verified using the operation response and the actual state of the data.

### 14.3. Offline Android

In offline mode, some operations may initially be performed on the device using local data and may not be recorded on the central server until synchronization occurs.

Important considerations when analyzing this workflow include:

- The source of serial number generation.
- Prevention of duplicate serials across multiple devices.
- Local data storage.
- The status of operations that have not yet been submitted.
- The timing and outcome of synchronization.
- Conflicts between local and central data.
- Differences between local controls and server-side controls.

The agent must not assume that the controls used in the online version are implemented identically in the offline version. Actual behavior must be established from the implementation of the relevant version.

### 14.4. API

When analyzing an API workflow, it must be determined whether the source system:

- Sends a request for Silo to generate a serial number; or
- Sends Silo a serial number that was generated by the source system.

In the example described for the Faradid project and the TeamYar system, the serial number and associated information are received from the source system. This example must not be generalized to all Silo APIs.

### 14.5. Windows and Web

Some registration or Gate capabilities were implemented in older versions as Windows Forms applications, while some capabilities have been migrated to the web in newer architectures.

The web version can support centrally managed server-side changes and reduce the need to update each user's local application separately.

However, the availability of a capability in the web or Windows version must be verified for the relevant version and project. The agent must not assume that all capabilities have been migrated to the web.

## 15. Logic of the Two Android Forms

### 15.1. Registration by Product Code

In the described form, the user selects a product and the required information, then reads RFID tags.

In this scenario, the number of tags read may determine the number of serials required for the current request.

- **Multi-tag:** Allows multiple tags to be read in a single process.
- **Single-tag:** Restricts the reading process to one tag.

These features relate to tag reading and must not be confused with the serial generation method or with online versus offline operation.

The number of tags read does not, by itself, prove how many serials were created or how many tags were successfully registered.

### 15.2. Registration by Product Code and Serial Number

In this form, a specific serial number already exists or is obtained from a source such as an IMEI, in addition to the product code.

The final serial may be identical to the input value or may be generated by combining it with other values according to a project-specific formula.

In the described logic, this form handles a single, specified serial number. The processing details and final registration behavior must be verified against the actual implementation.

These two forms must not be treated as equivalent merely because both support RFID tag reading.

## 16. Dynamic Fields

In the described architecture, dynamic fields may be defined in three categories:

### 16.1. Product Fields

Used to store general product attributes and information.

### 16.2. Serial Fields

Used to store attributes associated with a specific unit or serial record.

### 16.3. Operation Fields

Used to store information required when an operation is executed.

Dynamic fields may be editable, read-only, or calculated. The availability of these capabilities depends on the actual project implementation.

The purpose of a dynamic-field architecture is to reduce the need to create numerous fixed tables or columns for different attributes. However, the agent must not assume that all Silo forms or versions use the same structure.

In some projects, registration is performed using a handheld device, and the device's location information may also be recorded.

For example, in a vehicle-related project, capturing the device's GPS location during registration was required for security purposes.

This example is specific to that scenario. The agent must not conclude that GPS recording is mandatory for all Silo registration operations.

- Does the project capture location information?
- At what stage is the location recorded?
- Is capturing the location mandatory?
- If location data is unavailable, is the operation blocked or allowed to continue?

## 18. Analytical Examples

### Scenario 1: The Serial Exists in Print, but No Tag Record Exists

**Permitted conclusion:** The initial serial information exists, but no corresponding Tag record was found in the data examined.

**Not permitted:** Claiming that the tag has definitely been registered or that the label has definitely been printed.

### Scenario 2: The EPC Was Read, but No Tag Record Exists

**Permitted conclusion:** The identifier was received or read, but successful registration cannot be confirmed from the current data.

To identify the cause, the actual operation result and relevant data must be examined. The cause must not be guessed.

### Scenario 3: A Tag Record Exists, but No Warehouse Movement Was Found

**Permitted conclusion:** The existence of the tag record is confirmed, but the expected movement record was not confirmed in the data examined.

This does not necessarily mean that tag registration failed. The registration and warehouse movement stages may be separate, or the relevant workflow may create a different type of movement record.

### Scenario 4: Multiple Print Records Share the Same PrintActionId

**Permitted conclusion:** These records belong to the same request or operation group, according to the meaning of this identifier in the relevant implementation.

**Not permitted:** Declaring that all records in the group were successfully printed or registered without examining the corresponding data.

### Scenario 5: The Serial Was Received from an External System

**Permitted conclusion:** The input serial was provisioned by an external source.

To determine the final serial, it must be established whether the input is used as-is or processed by the project's formula.

### Scenario 6: Multiple Offline Devices Generate Serial Numbers

To evaluate the risk of duplicate serials, the serial allocation source, uniqueness mechanism, local storage, and synchronization rules must be examined.

The existence of a local identifier does not, by itself, guarantee that two devices cannot generate duplicate serials.

## 19. Internal Analysis and Response Rules for the Agent

1. First, determine whether the question concerns serial generation, initial record creation, printing, RFID reading, registration, or warehouse entry.
2. Do not treat these stages as interchangeable.
3. When verifying actual status, examine valid data for the relevant serial and tag rather than relying solely on conceptual documentation.
4. Do not treat the existence of a serial in Print as proof of registration.
5. Do not treat the presence of an EPC as proof of successful registration without verifying its association with the serial.
6. When warehouse workflow completion must be verified, also examine movement and destination information.
7. Interpret PrintActionId only within the scope of request grouping.
8. Do not invent custom formulas, database relationships, status meanings, or enabled controls without authoritative documentation.
9. When analyzing online and offline workflows, identify the relevant version and execution path.
10. Distinguish the number of serials from the quantity represented by each serial.
11. Interpret Multi-tag and Single-tag as tag-reading features, not as serial generation methods.
12. Do not generalize the behavior of one RFID or barcode-based project to another project.
13. Distinguish tag registration from completion of the warehouse workflow.
14. If sufficient information is unavailable, specify exactly which conclusion cannot be verified.
15. In ordinary user-facing responses, use operational language. Mention table names, column names, and internal implementation details only when explicitly providing a technical answer.
16. During troubleshooting, report only causes established by actual data or authoritative documentation. Do not list generic or speculative causes.
